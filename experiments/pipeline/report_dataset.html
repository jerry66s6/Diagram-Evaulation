The final pipeline, unchanged: two VLM judges (gpt-6-luna) score the 13 dimensions, and the classifier in classifier.pkl (gradient boosting trained on the human scores of HS001 to HS200) calls each diagram good or bad. The call is compared with the label in data.csv: negative (bad, one to four errors inserted on purpose) or positive (good, the original figure).

  • Arrow errors alone are mostly missed: a detached, reversed or erased arrow as the only error is caught in 11 of 59 diagrams. A faded label or low resolution alone is caught in 35% and 47% of the diagrams.
  • Missing or cut content is caught: a blanked label, a cropped edge or a removed box alone is caught in 100%, 94% and 89% of the diagrams.
  • Of the 545 diagrams with a single error, 175 are missed.

The run

78,000 judge calls (6,000 diagrams × 13 dimensions), 6 at a time, about 20 hours, about $23.5. 71 calls were refused by OpenAI's prompt filter (a false flag); all went through when re-run, so every diagram has all 13 scores.

Limits

  • The labels are the original ones. In the 300 study diagrams 7% of the originals already had a clear error; if that holds here, part of the 710 flagged originals are really bad, and the accuracy shown here is a little lower than it would be on corrected labels.
  • Each diagram was scored once by each judge; how much the scores move between runs was not measured here.
  • Several error kinds never appear alone, so their “only error” column is empty; single-error counts are small (10 to 55 diagrams per kind).
