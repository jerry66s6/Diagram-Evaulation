The pipeline: two VLM judges (gpt-6-luna) score the 13 dimensions, and gradient boosting trained on human scores calls each diagram good or bad.

What was wrong with the labels

Positive meant the original published figure, negative the figure with one to three errors inserted on purpose. 101 diagrams that looked suspicious (by the human scores, the judge scores, or either classifier) were looked at again, image and caption only, without seeing any score. The same standard was used for both labels: a diagram is bad if it has a clear error of the kinds that were inserted.

  • No negative was wrong. In all 38 suspicious negatives at least one inserted error can be seen or contradicts the caption.
  • 11 original figures already have a clear error and are relabeled bad, 6 of them in the test set.

Results on the corrected labels

Every classifier is retrained on the corrected labels of HS001 to HS200 with the same settings as before (200 trees, learning rate 0.03), so only the labels change. The judge scores are the saved ones; no judge was re-run.

  1. The pipeline gains most: accuracy 0.74 to 0.82, AUC 0.849 to 0.901. It calls 5 of the 6 bad test originals bad: part of what counted as good diagrams wrongly flagged were bad diagrams.
  2. The classifier trained on judge scores barely moves (accuracy 0.82 to 0.83); it calls only 2 of the 6 bad originals bad.
  3. Human scores stay the ceiling at 0.92 accuracy.

Limits

  • Only the suspicious diagrams were re-checked, by a model (Claude), not by human annotators; a human look at the 11 changes is still to do.
  • 100 test diagrams: one diagram moves accuracy by 1 point.
