The chosen version, d2, scores each correctness dimension directly: the model sees the image and caption and gives 1–10, with the scoring idea written into the prompt. It replaced the sketch-and-formula method (v1). Highlighted cells are the best value in each row; brown cells are the human reference.

Setup

  • 100 diagrams, HS001–HS100: 43 easy, 26 medium, 31 hard; 53 clean and 47 with inserted errors.
  • One human rating per diagram on 1–10 (rater_1: 76, rater_2: 16, rater_3: 8). Judge scores on the formula's 0–1 scale are multiplied by 10.
  • Overall correctness: humans and the direct versions use the mean of the five dimensions; the sketch + formula versions use the pipeline's own overall score.
  • An error counts as caught when its dimension falls below 8 (with the formula, one detected error gives 7.5). A false alarm is a clean diagram that humans rated 9 or 10 but the judge scored below 8.

Conclusions

  • Direct scoring beats sketch + formula on every dimension. Overall Spearman 0.53 → 0.78, MAE 1.18 → 0.69, with less than half the input tokens and a fifth of the output tokens.
  • The formula, not the model, was the main problem. v1 detected most inserted errors, but one detected error usually cost 2.5 points (score 7.5), while humans often drop a dimension to 4–6 for a clear error; and clean real figures lost points because the caption sketch did not match how they were drawn (exclusion boxes chained into the main flow, one arrow serving several boxes).
  • d2's sentences helped beyond noise on Details and Connectivity, the two dimensions they targeted.
  • Adding the sketch back (d3) did not help: overall the same as d2, Connectivity worse (more false alarms), at about 1.6 times the cost.
  • d4–d6, from the d2 mistakes: best so far is d6 (overall Spearman 0.79, MAE 0.71). Layout gained most: d2 still called tightly cropped figures cut off, and saying that only sliced text or an incomplete box is cut off lifted Layout Spearman 0.50 → 0.64 while still catching every inserted crop.
  • The study's display size explains much of the Legibility gap. Figures were shown fitted to a panel about 660×360 pixels, so large figures looked far smaller to raters than to the model. Telling the model the scale ranks Legibility better but overshoots the level (bias +0.53 in d2, -0.92 in d6).
  • d7–d8, general rules without sizes: d8 is the best general prompt (overall Spearman 0.79, MAE 0.66, false alarms on clean diagrams 14 vs 24 for d2). Presence gained most from taking "visibly present" literally (Spearman 0.59 → 0.73). Legibility wordings without numbers score like d2 at every display size: the judge sees the full-resolution file and a prompt alone does not close that gap.
  • Sentences that excuse a kind of error backfire: in d4, "typos do not count" made the judge pass real wrong labels and "a label in the wrong box is a Details problem" excused real arrow errors; d5 dropped both.

Caveats

  • The prompt was tuned on these 100 diagrams; the claim has to be confirmed once on HS101–HS300.
  • Each diagram has one human rater, so agreement between humans cannot be measured. Humans often lowered every dimension of a diagram that had any error, even dimensions with no inserted error; the judge is meant to score each dimension on its own, which caps the agreement it can reach.
